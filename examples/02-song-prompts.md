# Song prompts — LAST TRAIN, FIRST SNOW

12 tracks. Append the global prompt to each per-track line before submitting; a fully
concatenated example is at the bottom.

## Global prompt

> Instrumental slow-travel folk, no vocals, no spoken word, no choir, no whistling, no
> bluegrass banjo breakdown, no celtic reel, no country twang, no cinematic trailer build,
> no EDM drop. Fingerpicked steel-string acoustic guitar, felt piano, quietly brushed
> drums, upright bass, one warm analog pad, subtle tape texture. Make the first 5–10
> seconds musically attractive and immediately playable. Keep the track complete,
> unhurried, warm, and quietly hopeful.

## Per-track lines

**01. Ticket Machine, 6:40pm** — 84 BPM, A minor. Open with A3–C4–D4–G4 fingerpicked on
steel-string guitar within 4 seconds; let felt piano shadow only the final interval and
leave the phrase unresolved. Brushed snare, soft kick, upright bass, faint room tone.
A small private moment before a journey, not an opening fanfare.

**02. Steam on the Window** — 76 BPM, C major. Warm felt piano chord idea within 5
seconds answered by two guitar harmonics; do not state the four-note sequence. Slow
brushed groove, rounded bass, one pad underneath, slightly dampened high end as though
heard through a coat.

**03. Sleepers Under Snow** — 72 BPM, D minor. Low felt piano in close voicings within 6
seconds, unrelated to the project motif. Long pauses between phrases, deep soft kick,
patient bass, guitar entering late and quietly. Weight and stillness, not sadness.

**04. The Junction at Dusk** — 88 BPM, G major. Guitar and piano arriving together on the
first beat within 4 seconds, a bright interlocking figure. Steady brushed drums, walking
upright bass, warm pad, small tape flutter. The moment the line splits and picks up speed.

**05. Someone Else's Newspaper** — 92 BPM, E minor. Brushed groove established first with
a clipped guitar figure over it within 5 seconds. Nimble bass, muted piano stabs, dry
drums. Lightly curious, mildly comic, never busy. No explicit motif.

**06. Frost on the Rails** — 70 BPM, B♭ major. One warm analog pad, then felt piano
entering within 7 seconds with suspended ninth voicings. Brushed cymbal only, very deep
kick, long bass notes, high guitar harmonics like light on ice. No drums until halfway.

**07. Two Stops Early** — 86 BPM, F major. Fingerpicked guitar ostinato within 5 seconds
carrying a fragment of A3–C4–D4–G4 with the third note delayed and the last one missing;
let upright bass imply the absent note. Tight brushed snare, ghost notes, felt piano
answering sparsely. Quiet recognition, no emphasis.

**08. Vending Machine Hum** — 80 BPM, A minor. Clipped repeating felt-piano figure within
4 seconds, unrelated to the motif. Dry drums, nimble bass ostinato, one sustained low
electrical drone under the whole track, tape hiss. Fluorescent light, late, slightly
absurd.

**09. Fields Going White** — 68 BPM, C major. Spacious felt piano within 8 seconds with
generous space around each phrase, brushed drums entering gradually, soft bass, wide pad,
airy guitar harmonics. The stereo field opens as the landscape does. No explicit motif.

**10. Last Announcement** — 78 BPM, G minor. Dark felt-piano harmony within 6 seconds,
pauses between phrases, low kick, rim clicks, patient bass, suspended tension that never
quite resolves. The sound of a platform emptying.

**11. Boots on the Platform** — 90 BPM, D major. Bright fingerpicked guitar within 4
seconds, crisp brushed beat, warm upright bass, short piano ostinato. Arrival, cold air,
forward motion. No explicit motif.

**12. Going Home in the Dark** — 74 BPM, moving from A minor toward A major. Most
complete reharmonized statement of A3–C4–D4–G4 on guitar and felt piano together within 5
seconds, then pad and soft harmonics widen the return. Brushes, rounded bass, open
major-seventh ending. Resolves without slowing into ambience.

> Create an original instrumental slow-travel folk track titled "Ticket Machine, 6:40pm,"
> 84 BPM, A minor. Open with A3–C4–D4–G4 fingerpicked on steel-string guitar within 4
> seconds; let felt piano shadow only the final interval and leave the phrase unresolved.
> Brushed snare, soft kick, upright bass, faint room tone. A small private moment before a
> journey, not an opening fanfare. No vocals, no spoken word, no choir, no whistling, no
> bluegrass banjo breakdown, no celtic reel, no country twang, no cinematic trailer build,
> no EDM drop. Fingerpicked steel-string acoustic guitar, felt piano, quietly brushed
> drums, upright bass, one warm analog pad, subtle tape texture. Keep the track complete,
> unhurried, warm, and quietly hopeful. About three minutes.
